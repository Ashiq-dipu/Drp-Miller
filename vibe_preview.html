About Dr. Miller · Keynote Speaker

From the projects of Rochester to executive leadership.

Dr. Paul Miller is a transformational leadership and resilience speaker, bestselling author, former CEO, educator, professor, coach, and advocate for educational opportunity and community empowerment. His journey began in the projects of Rochester, New York, where he grew up in poverty in a single-parent household — his mother white, his biological father Puerto Rican and Black. His childhood exposed him to father absence, violence, economic hardship, questions of identity, and limited opportunity.

Rather than allowing those circumstances to define his destination, he developed a mindset centered on perseverance, education, discipline, mentorship, and personal responsibility. He went on to become a college athlete, teacher, coach, assistant principal, principal, school reform leader, CEO, author, and national speaker. Across more than 26 years in urban education and organizational leadership, Dr. Miller has worked to improve outcomes for young people and to develop the adults and systems responsible for their success.

As the former CEO of Green Tech Charter Schools, he led work focused on educational opportunity and achievement, particularly for Black young men; his leadership materials highlight reported graduation rates of approximately 95% in 2017 and 2018. He also teaches leadership development to aspiring and practicing educational leaders through St. John Fisher University's graduate educational leadership program. He understands adversity personally, leadership professionally, and organizational change through the responsibility of leading people and producing results.

Signature Frameworks & Development Tools

Practical tools that change behavior.

Every keynote is backed by repeatable frameworks audiences can apply the moment they leave the room.

Championship Mindset

A champion leans into fear, shows up even when motivation is low, develops mental toughness, sees possibilities, prioritizes progress over perfection, and approaches success as a marathon rather than a sprint.

WAGAL

Welcome All Challenges · Anticipate Obstacles · Greatness Comes from Effort · Appreciate Criticism · Let Others Inspire You — a five-letter framework for high-performance habits.

Reverse the Curse

Challenge the tendency to turn temporary failure into permanent identity. Instead of “I failed because I'm incapable,” examine preparation, effort, strategy, support, and opportunities for improvement.

Before I Can Do for You, I Must Do for Me

Self-leadership precedes effective leadership of others. Understand your identity, beliefs, wounds, values, and behavior before attempting to influence a team or community.

Leadership Authenticity

Know Yourself. Lead Yourself. Then Lead Others. Use personal reflection, leadership-style analysis, values alignment, emotional awareness, coaching, and accountability to build credibility and trust.

The Good Me vs. The Hood Me

The Good Me represents intentional choices, purpose, discipline, compassion, and accountability. The Hood Me represents reactive decisions shaped by fear, ego, pressure, or immediate gratification.

Four Pillars of Improvement

Building Relationships · Delivery of Quality Instruction · Usage of Data to Improve Instruction · Accountability for All Stakeholders — adaptable to customer relationships, quality service, performance measurement, and organizational accountability.

Mission, Vision & Values Alignment

Help teams clarify what they stand for, what they promise stakeholders, how those promises translate into daily behavior, and what evidence demonstrates whether they are being fulfilled.
